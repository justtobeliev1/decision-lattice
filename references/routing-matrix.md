# Routing Matrix

Use this file to choose a compact starting bundle before opening the full model catalog.

## Decision Axes

Classify the problem across these axes:

- `goal`: truth finding, option choice, intervention, coordination, allocation, risk defense
- `stakes`: low, medium, high
- `reversibility`: easy to reverse, costly to reverse, hard to reverse
- `system shape`: simple, coupled, adaptive, opaque
- `actor count`: solo, few actors, many actors
- `uncertainty`: low, moderate, high, tail-heavy
- `time shape`: immediate, compounding, path-locking, slow feedback

## Default Bundles

| Situation | Default bundle | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is a claim or diagnosis actually true? | first principles, deduction/induction/abduction, falsifiability, map is not the territory, Occam's razor | cleans up assumptions, evidence, and overfitting |
| Choose between a few concrete options | expected value, prospect theory, inversion, opportunity cost, second-order thinking | balances upside, downside, tradeoffs, and consequences |
| Intervene in a messy system | feedback loops, emergence, leverage points, entropy, path dependence | models response, adaptation, and lock-in |
| Design strategy in a competitive or social field | game theory, incentives, comparative advantage, network effects, second-order thinking | captures reactions, positioning, and coordination |
| Build or scale a product, process, or market | compounding, scaling laws, diminishing returns, marginal thinking, network effects | handles nonlinear growth and allocation quality |
| Make a call with existential downside | inversion, black swan, antifragility, ergodicity, bounded rationality | prioritizes survival, robustness, and realistic limits |
| Design org roles, workflows, or collaboration | incentives, comparative advantage, game theory, social construction, bounded rationality | predicts behavior inside structures and stories |
| Understand why a system is stuck | feedback loops, path dependence, leverage points, paradigm shift, dialectics | surfaces reinforcing traps and framing mistakes |
| Research a new space with weak evidence | abduction, information theory, falsifiability, Dunning-Kruger and circle of competence, latticework of mental models | supports hypothesis generation and calibration |
| Review roadmap, portfolio, or resource allocation | opportunity cost, marginal thinking, diminishing returns, compounding, second-order thinking | optimizes scarce attention and capital |

## Coverage Rule

For medium or high-stakes decisions, try to cover at least three of these four lenses:

- `epistemic`: what is true and how sure are we
- `systemic`: how the environment will react
- `social`: how incentives and other actors change the outcome
- `temporal`: how risk, compounding, or ruin evolve over time

## Challenger Models

Add one of these when the answer feels too neat:

- `paradigm shift` when the whole framing may be outdated
- `dialectics` when two strong perspectives are colliding
- `map is not the territory` when abstractions may be hiding reality
- `bounded rationality` when the plan assumes superhuman decision-makers
- `black swan` when base rates hide catastrophic tails

## Stop Rules

Stop adding models when:

- a new model adds only stylistic variation
- the recommendation stops changing
- the evidence bottleneck is clearer than the reasoning bottleneck
- the user needs action more than elaboration
